The shop

We make waterproof products that you can trust in the heart of one of the wettest places on earth.

The workshop is in Kake, a small fishing town you can reach only by boat or plane. We build each bag one by one. We answer the phone, respond to emails, and fulfill the orders. When we aren’t building bags, we offset the high cost of living here by gathering food, gardening on our property, fishing, and hunting. In Kake we get an average of 132 inches of rain a year, against a national average of 38.

Backpack

Dry Daypack

Medium $440 · Large $455

The Dry Daypack is designed for the outdoors person who recreates in rainy and wet climates. An offset, over-the-top waterproof zipper opens the main compartment, which is fully submersible. The smaller front pocket is rainproof. The fully padded back and hip suspension comes off. An adjustable sternum strap connects the shoulder straps.

Inside, foam runs across the bottom and up the back, and a small hanging pocket holds keys or a wallet. Two lash straps on the side will hold a rod tube. The medium has about 1,500 cubic inches in the main compartment. The large has about 1,650. Both front pockets are about 150 cubic inches.

Fit, in the shop’s words

They recommend a medium for people up to 5′8″ with a more slender build, and a large if you are over 5′8″, longer in the torso, or a more stocky build. The normal waistband covers up to 60 inches. A larger or smaller one is something to note when you order. If you want help choosing, they ask you to say so in the comments.

02 — The workshop

Made with intention.

Sagebrush Dry Gear was founded in the early 2000s in Dillon, Montana, by Robbie and Elaine Garrett. When the company’s contract for making waterproof gear for a large outdoor company was diverted to factories outside of the US, the founders moved the company to Southeast Alaska, where they could live off grid and create bags in one of the rainiest places on earth.

The company is in Kake now. Current owner John Peterka relocated from Bozeman, Montana in 2017, after working for various outdoor companies. He was drawn to Sagebrush for the craftsmanship, the American-made gear, and the way of life Kake offers.

For over twenty years the shop has made small-batch dry bags with hot air and radio frequency welding: thermal bonded seams, built to stay ultra dry and to last. Makers here are artists and outdoor enthusiasts. They test the work in the same weather they live in — fishing, camping, boating, hunting, biking, kayaking, hiking, and foraging.

Our story How we make our bags

What sets the seam apart

  1. Cloth. Nylon pack cloth, custom milled in Northborough, Massachusetts. Polyurethane coated and embossed for abrasion resistance.
  2. Zippers. Custom made to their specifications in Germany by TIZIP.
  3. Seams. A proprietary “hot air sewing” process uses radio frequency welding and hot air to make double thermal bonded seams. They require no stitching and no taping.
  4. The parts that are not the shell. Suspension systems and other non-waterproof components are sewn in house.
  5. The bath. Each bag is submerged in a water bath and inspected for leaks before it is sold and shipped.

A remote island makes materials expensive to bring in. Shop scraps become the small anchor-point pieces and pull tabs, so less of the cloth is thrown out.

04 — Repair

A lifetime warranty, with the limits written down.

“We believe in making products that stand the test of time. Because of this, we offer a lifetime warranty against any defect in materials or workmanship in our products.”

The warranty does not cover improper care, accidents, normal wear and tear, or the natural breakdown of materials over an extended time. Repairs outside that line have a fee. The shop asks you to write first — a photo and a description — to john@sagebrushdry.com. Bags sent without that conversation will not be fixed. Take the suspension off and clean the bag before it goes. Repairs are done in batches, at cost, when several have piled up, because the same small staff is also making bags.
